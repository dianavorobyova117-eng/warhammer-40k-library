Q: How does climbing and dropping work across different
parts of terrain or different terrain features during one action?

A: Total up the combined vertical distance during the action
before rounding increments. For climbing, an operative can
continue its climb so long as the next terrain is within 1"
horizontally and 3" vertically.

In example 1, the operative moves over two different terrain
features and moves a total of 2½" vertically up before
dropping 1" once. It can climb the second terrain feature
without dropping, as it’s within 1" of it before it needs to drop
from the first.

In example 2, the operative climbs one section of the terrain
feature 2" up, then continues its climb another 1½" vertically.
The distance is only rounded up once, meaning the operative
uses 4" of its movement allowance towards the vertical
distance of the climb.
